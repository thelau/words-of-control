# Direction v2 — appraisal as a sequence

Draft for discussion, 2026-09-23. Replaces the "one continuous particle event" model of SPEC §6.
Inspiration: references/craft/video-d.mp4 (monolith, data → relief, hard cuts, black), Ryoji Ikeda
*datamatics*, the four reviews in docs/reviews/. Not a clone: see "What makes it ours".

## Stance

New media art on demand: text → a sequenced, controlled piece. But **nothing is generated by an AI**.
The only AI (Jev) *appraises*; it never makes an image or a sound. Every image and sound comes from
authored generators, arranged in an authored grammar. The machine's judgement *performs* the work,
like a score played by a blind interpreter. Authorship stays with the artist.

- **Grammar (fixed, authored):** appraisal → verdict clips → black with reverb → rest. The constancy is the ritual.
- **Vocabulary (the clip library):** finite, curated, each clip crafted to gallery standard in isolation.
- **Performer (the director):** chooses, orders, parameterises and cuts from the judgement, deterministically.
- **Live:** rendered in real time from one clock; each performance exists once, for one visitor.

## The shape of one loop

| Phase | Duration | Image | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rest** | open | Near-black. Barely-there matter, grain of the film, the room. | The drone (the plate at rest). |
| **Typing** | open | The word, centred. The dark stirs *very* slightly around it — never a starfield. | Key clicks on the housing; the drone leans in. |
| **Wait** | 0.1–0.5 s | Everything holds. | The drone holds. |
| **Appraisal** | 1.5–3 s | The machine's reading of the word, shown as raw structure: bit-fields, barcodes, scan lines, a single line — Ikeda-rigour, monochrome + one accent. Fast hard cuts. | Data sonification: sine clicks, bursts, pulses — each bar/bit is a sound. Robotic voice chorus enters here (see Voices). |
| **Verdict** | 4–10 s | A series of generated **micro-clips** (1.5–4 s each) that together synthesize the emotion: different particles, textures, surfaces, fields, light. Chosen, parameterised and cut by a *director* from the analysis. | Each clip carries its own matching sound; hard cuts in sound with the image. |
| **Release** | 2–4 s | **Cut to black.** No "big bang", no particles flying home. | The last sound rings on in a long **reverb tail** over the black, sinking into the drone; the drone keeps the memory. |
| **Rest** | — | Cursor returns. | Drone, now slightly changed. |

The rhythm (how many shots, how long each, how long the black between) comes from the analysis: an
indifferent word gets one long, slow, almost-empty shot ("a lazy afternoon"); an anguished one gets
many short ones; low confidence produces false starts and shots that don't resolve.

## Clip library + director (Laurent, 2026-09-23)

- **Clip** = a self-contained generator (WebGPU scene + matching Web Audio voice) with one interface:
  `(analysis, seed, duration) → shot`. Every clip is composed around the centre.
- **Library now (2026-09-23, night): the appraisal gone 3D.** Points of light placed by the word's own data
  (src/render/shaders/field.wgsl), fine dust plus a few bokeh carriers, a warm/cool depth ramp and the
  appraisal's accent: *landscape* (spectrum → rows of streaming lines), *city* (barcode → bars with bright caps),
  *lattice* (bits → voxels), *cloud* (scatter → a path through the return map), *tube* (line → one closed
  curve), *drift* (the void). Plus *relief* (data → surface, video-d) and *chladni* (the word's bytes as a sound
  shaping sand). Each shot is covered by several camera angles (wide, grazing, inside the formation), each
  aimed and focused on a real data point.
- **Sound** follows the same data: the formations are sonified in the appraisal's palette (tones, clicks,
  pulses, FM blips, a high grain for the points), over 1–3 beds (src/audio/beds.ts), with deep beating
  impacts on cuts and, for dark words, on every shot. No voices after the appraisal. No swooshes.
- **Director** = picks 1–7 clips by affinity to the analysis (material, motion, emotion, texture…) with a
  weighted draw, sets their parameters, orders them and cuts them to the edit pace (dark/violent → fast,
  calm → long takes). Seeded by the word + the answers + a per-performance salt; it remembers the last
  performances and avoids repeating them (except for idle words).
- Grow the library over time; each clip is polished in isolation in the harness.

## Point to decide: "no text, no numbers" (SPEC §1.1, §1.9)

The Ikeda idea puts the analysis *on screen*. Three levels, from strictest to loosest:

- **A. Structure only (recommended).** The real Jev data (distributions, scores, confidences, the
  input's own bytes) rendered as bars, bit-grids, barcodes, scan lines, histograms without labels. The
  data is genuinely there, exact, but illegible as language — the machine *cannot speak*, it can only
  show its appraisal as raw form. Keeps rule 1.
- **B. Raw numerals, no words.** As A, plus columns of raw floats/hex/bytes flashing by (like video-d).
  Never labels, never emotion names. Bends rule 1 ("no numbers").
- **C. Readable analysis.** Labels, emotion names. Breaks the concept; not recommended.

## Voices

"A chorus of robotic voices that becomes noise." No AI voices. Options, all real-time in the browser:

- **Formant choir (pure Web Audio).** Buzz/pulse sources → vowel formant filters (a, e, i, o, u, and
  in-between vowels), many voices, each tuned to the plate's harmonics. Wordless: vowels and
  consonant-like noise bursts only. Keeps "no words" absolute.
- **Retro formant speech (non-AI):** SAM (Software Automatic Mouth, 1982, tiny JS port) or eSpeak-ng
  compiled to WASM. Very robotic, deterministic. Could *vocalise the data* — e.g. many voices reading
  raw numbers or phonemes at once, layered until it becomes a chorus of noise (Kraftwerk *Nummern* ×
  Ikeda). Any single voice might be momentarily intelligible → bends "no words".
- **Hybrid:** SAM/eSpeak phonemes (not words) → granulated, pitched to the plate, 8–32 voices.

## What we analyse (point 1)

One Jev call still (batching barely changes latency). Plus things measured locally, never stored.

**From Jev (proposal, ~45 questions):**

- *Affect:* emotion distribution (9) + confidence; valence; arousal; dominance (in control ↔ overwhelmed).
- *Sense and matter:* material (metal, glass, stone, sand, water, ice, smoke, fire, wood, paper, cloth,
  skin, plastic, air, light); texture (smooth, grainy, crystalline, liquid, powdery, fibrous, cracked);
  surface (matte, glossy, rough, soft); which sense it evokes most (sight, sound, touch, smell, taste).
- *Form:* shape (round, jagged, flowing, splintered, knotted, flat, spiral); symmetry (ordered ↔ chaotic);
  density (sparse ↔ crowded); scale; weight; hardness; temperature; light; colour (choice from a fixed
  palette, never shown as a word).
- *Motion and time:* motion (rising, falling, spreading, contracting, circling, trembling, still,
  breaking); speed; rhythm (steady, pulsing, stuttering, one strike, dwindling); time (past, present,
  future, timeless); duration (instant ↔ endless); season; time of day.
- *Sound:* loudness; pitch (low ↔ high); the sound of the word itself (bouba ↔ kiki); noise ↔ tone.
- *Relation:* distance (intimate ↔ public); who (I, you, we, they, nobody); tension; loneliness;
  nature ↔ machine ↔ city ↔ body; sacred ↔ profane; childlike ↔ ancient.
- *Speech act:* statement, question, command, exclamation, name, greeting, insult-free swear, nonsense;
  sincere ↔ ironic; language / script (best-effort).
- *Accents:* violence, loss, closeness, absurd. *Safety* as calibrated.

**Measured locally (instant, never stored):**

- The string: length, letters vs spaces vs symbols, vowels/consonants, repeated letters, script(s),
  capitals, emoji; its bytes (UTF-8) — the most literal data there is.
- *How it was typed:* speed, hesitations, backspaces, the rhythm between keys. The machine appraises
  the gesture as well as the word.

## Mapping, so output feels connected

- **Material → rendering mode of the verdict shots** (glass → sharp refraction/crystal fracture; sand →
  lit grain field; stone → relief; smoke → soft volume; water → liquid ripples; metal → specular lines…).
- **Motion → what happens inside a shot** (rise, fall, spread, contract, break, tremble, stillness).
- **Shape/symmetry → form** (jagged vs round displacement, mirrored vs chaotic).
- **Emotion → light and palette** (one accent colour at most, warmth, contrast), not the form.
- **Rhythm/arousal/speed → the edit** (number of shots, cut timing, black between).
- **Confidence → resolution** (clean final frame vs false starts, unresolved shots, suspended chords).
- **Typing gesture → the appraisal phase** (hesitations appear in the data; backspaces as erased bits).
- **The input's bytes → the data imagery and the sound's rhythm** (exact, deterministic).
- **Sound ↔ image:** one clock, one set of parameters; every cut, line and grain is audible.

## What makes it ours (not Ikeda, not video-d)

- The data shown is *the judgment of your word*, happening now: you see the machine reading you.
- The centre rule holds: every shot is composed around the point where you typed (a slab, an aperture,
  a field centred on it).
- The indifference mode (time passing, warmth, nothing to see) — the machine can shrug.
- The drone remembers: the room accumulates the day's words.
- A refusal is a hard, silent cut — the one moment with no appraisal at all.

## Species (step 8): the second word must not look like the first

A visitor's second word looked "the same" even when the reading was different: every verdict was one
species — points of light on black — and people read species at a glance, behaviour only later. So the
verdict now comes in species that differ at first sight, and two performances in a row never share one
(director.ts `Species`, `lastSpecies`; the name and the void stay points, their clip is their meaning):

- **points** — the data formations, the relief, the sand (everything before step 8).
- **ink** — the reading gone liquid (ink.wgsl): a real fluid (advection, vorticity, pressure projection,
  256² velocity, 1024² dye). It is poured from the appraisal's last barcode (a question's: its line) — its bits as strokes of ink, not ruled
  columns: each bar its own length, height and weight, the whole reading tilted at the word's own angle and bent, then
  fed by the word: one source per byte, laid out in the word's shape (ring, wave, spiral, tree, knot, one
  point, a scatter, a low line), each firing on the word's beat and pushing the way it moves (grief falls,
  joy rises, fear trembles, love circles; a still word's drops just open). Matter is viscosity and weight
  (stone thick, water curls, smoke rises and thins, fire rises and burns away). Mood is pigment, never
  inversion: negative colourless, neutral cool with the density's isolines drawn exact, positive in its
  palette with a warm glow and glitter. Heard (clips.ts ink()): each drop a soft tone blooming with the
  push of water behind it, on the same beats, panned where it falls, over dark moving water.
  The first choice of a session goes by how liquid the reading is (`inkiness`), then the species alternate.

Next species (one step each): solids (sculpted forms, chrome/porcelain/stone), threads (long glowing
lines), light (colour fields, almost no objects). With four or more, the reading picks among those not
just seen, so it stops being a plain alternation.
