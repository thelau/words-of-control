# Brief — simulated visitors experiencing *Words of Control* (2026-10-04)

You are one visitor (your persona is in your instructions) at an exhibition. In a dark room there is a large screen, a
comfortable chair and a keyboard. The screen says YOUR WORDS. You type something, press Enter, and the piece performs
for 15–35 seconds — image and sound — then cuts to black and waits for the next person.

What happens (you do not know this as a visitor; read it only to understand the frames): one call to an AI (Jev)
answers 45 questions about the words — how positive, intense, violent, nostalgic… its colour, its material, what kind
of thing it is. A 9 × 5 grid fills with those 45 answers, each a little chart of how 993 ordinary reference words
answered (white ticks) and where your words fall (the bright line). The answers that set your words apart fill with
your words' colour; the rest go dark. Then, on a beat (faster for intense words), the answers that matter are merged,
and a few 3D views show your words among the reference words. It ends on one answer in plain words ("MANY MEANINGS",
"COMPLETELY SINCERE"), a dot plot of where the reference words stand on it, your words among them, and the three
nearest reference words on it. Sound: a low drone while typing; little tones as the grid fills; a sequencer built
from the answers that matter (dark words: clicks and blips, fast; bright words: softer, a chord); a short low tone at
the end. Nothing is AI-generated except the 45 answers.

**Your three performances** (real answers from the AI, real renders): for each of your words, the contact sheet
`docs/captures/sim/sheet-<slug>.png` and every frame `docs/captures/sim/<slug>-NN-<moment>.png` (2880 × 1800 screens,
in time order; "stand" is the ending). `<slug>` is the words with non-letters as `_` (e.g. `i_cheated_on_her`). Look
at the frames of your own words properly — open the full frames, not just the sheets — as the visitor who typed them.

## Answer as that visitor (under ~500 words, plain language, honest — not polite)

1. For each of your words: what did the piece seem to *say* about them, in one sentence, before reading the small
   labels closely? Then after reading them?
2. Which moments felt **connected** to what you typed, and which felt **arbitrary, generic or wrong**? Be specific
   (the frame, the label, the colour, the nearest words, the ending's answer).
3. **Correlation score** for each of your words, 1–5: 1 = it could have been anyone's words, 5 = it was clearly about
   mine.
4. Were your three performances **different enough** from each other to feel personal?
5. Did you **enjoy** it? Would you type another word, and what would you type? What one change would make it feel
   more about you?

Write your answers to the file named in your instructions, then reply with your three scores and one line.
