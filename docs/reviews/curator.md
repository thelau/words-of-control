# Words of Control: curator's review (build of 2026-09-23)

## 1. Verdict

The frame is already a work: a black room, a cursor, one or two words, a refusal that cuts instantly, a drone that remembers what the screen forgets. What happens inside the frame is still mostly a demo. The concept is a machine that "cannot see, hear, or speak, only appraise", but across the nine sheets it *celebrates*: every reaction climaxes in a bright central sun and full-frame radiance, and six of the nine share one concentric-ring template. The concept asks for judgement: cold, partial, sometimes wrong. The build delivers generosity and spectacle. Anger and sadness point to the piece it could be. Awe, tender and fear point to the screensaver. The fix is mostly subtraction.

## 2. What is already strong

- **The ritual.** A cursor on black, typing that is never melodic, Enter as the only verb.
- **The BARRED cut** (0 ms, silent). This is the most conceptually precise moment in the spec. A judgement with no language in it.
- **Drone memory.** "The screen forgets instantly; the room does not" is the best line in the document.
- **One population, always returning home.**
- **Anger** follows the approved storyboard (flash, jagged ring, streaks, embers). Its decay frame, with uneven hot embers on black, is the best still in the set.
- **Sadness** has the only genuinely *new* image: a ring that sheds downward.
- **Playful's** light-painted ribbons read almost like handwriting. In a work whose rule is "no words", a machine that *almost* writes is a rich tension. Keep it and make it deliberate.

## 3. What reads as cliché or works against the concept

**Across all nine:**
- **The central sun.** It appears at every ignition and most peaks. It is the "AI orb" of product launches, and it makes the machine a *source* of radiance rather than a blind evaluator. Your craft references (video b) are irises. A machine that cannot see should not be drawn as a glowing eye.
- **The ring template.** Calm, awe, sadness, joy, anxiety and playful all resolve into concentric bands. At a glance, awe and calm are the same target, and so are joy and anxiety.
- **The typing frame** is a full-frame warp-speed radial field, louder than several reactions and identical for every word.
- **Return frames expose the machinery.** Joy returns as a rectangle, fear as a hard-edged white box, tender as a flattened oval. The return is the moment every watcher sees, and it is the least crafted.
- **Emission over matter.** `sand-rings.jpg` is dark, raked and side-lit, with rings *carved* into grains. The build almost never looks like matter. It looks like additive light summing to cream.

**Per emotion:**
- **Calm:** a hypnotic iris-vortex. It is well made, but it is the meditation-app screensaver: too bright and too busy for calm.
- **Tender:** a glowing ball in haze that reads as "sun" or "loading". Nothing about it feels held or close.
- **Joy:** a golden burst plus rings, which is fireworks. Structurally it is anger in gold.
- **Awe:** an archery target or sonar ping. The spec says "stillness is the point". Awe should be the *least* designed image: scale and dark.
- **Sadness:** the right idea, but the neon ring is logo-hard, and the tears fall as a rectangular curtain, closer to a waterfall than weeping.
- **Fear:** the opposite of the spec. The spec calls for a tiny trembling knot in a void, and the build gives a big bright sun. Fear should be the darkest reaction and the hardest to see.
- **Anxiety:** the wobbling contour lines are promising. The glowing amber donut makes it stable and warm, which is exactly what anxiety isn't.
- **Playful:** the ribbons are good. The pink cloud and rings behind them are decoration, and the pink scribble-box return is the weakest frame in the set.
- **Anger:** drop the pale grey shockwave in the release frame and the pink hairball in the return. End on the embers.

## 4. Top-7 recommendations (ranked)

1. **Kill the default sun.** Show a central emissive core only where it is earned (anger ignition, perhaps joy). Make ignition a *darkening* or contraction as often as a flash. The appraisal happens *to* the dust, not *from* a core.
2. **Halve the light budget and make it matter.** Cap peaks so that no more than about 35% of pixels come near white. Push the matter layer toward sand-rings: raking light, shadowed grains, grooves. Keep emissive sparks for anger, joy and ignition only.
3. **Break the ring template.** Give each emotion one topology and forbid it to the others. Rings belong to calm alone, as slowly raked grooves rather than glowing bands. Awe is a slow withdrawal: grains recede and the frame grows emptier. Tender is a small cupped cluster that dims inward. Anxiety is a ring that never closes, with a gap that migrates. Fear is a knot under 5% of screen height, with everything else at rest-black.
4. **Quiet the typing charge by about 80%.** A slight inward lean, a few grains brightening near the cursor. Whispering while typing, then speaking once after Enter, is the whole pacing.
5. **Make the return the signature.** No rectangles or ovals: each grain takes its own curved path home, staggered by distance, like an exhalation that is the same for all nine emotions. That shared exhale is where "one instrument" is proven.
6. **Let the machine be wrong, and show confidence.** Low confidence should look *unresolved* (a hesitation, a false start, two behaviours alternating), not just noisier. Legibility should come from the gap between word and reaction, not from matching them. Stop testing with emotion names.
7. **Design for the room.** Use a large matte rear-projection or a deep-black OLED at human scale. Put the keyboard on a plinth angled away from the queue, with small type, so watchers see *only* the appraisal and guess the word. That guessing is the social engine of the work.

**On sound:** the machine has no voice, so the reaction should not sing or emote. It should be the **sound of a verdict being registered**, closer to a stamp, a relay or a struck material than a melody. Keep it sparse, physical and short, and let its tail sink *into the drone* instead of resolving by itself. The drone is the room's memory, and the reaction is the machine's mark on it. Low confidence should leave something audibly unfinished for the next visitor. It must never sound like reward music, or the work becomes a slot machine.

## 5. Study

- **Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Room* / *Voice Tunnel*.** One visitor's input becomes a public trace the queue inherits and overwrites.
- **Ryoji Ikeda (*test pattern*) against Zimoun's sound sculptures.** Ikeda for monochrome rigour at architectural scale. Zimoun for matter over light, with simple physical repetition that feels alive. The sand register sits between them.
- **Trevor Paglen, *Sight Machine* and *From "Apple" to "Anomaly"*.** Machines that classify without understanding, made uncanny without dashboards. It sharpens what "only appraise" means, and why a reaction should sometimes feel unjust.
