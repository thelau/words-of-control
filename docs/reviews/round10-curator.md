# Round 10: curator review (the grid, step 28+)

Material: all 8 sheets; full frames of war (filled, tiles), fuck (marked), love (cloud), mother (network full,
stand), nothing (map full), to be or not to be (axis), spoon and i miss you (stand). Read against GRID.md and
round8-curator.md.

## What improved since round 8

Most of my round-8 notes were acted on. The norm is now visible. Every cell's strip shows the 61 words, so "war ·
order 0.067" reads as *against whom*, and the stand says it plainly: "61 REFERENCE WORDS, CHOSEN BY THE ARTIST". The
company is now the climax. "i miss you", nearest on all 45 answers: **WHY DID YOU LEAVE ME WITHOUT SAYING GOODBYE ·
LONELINESS · SORRY**. That line is the best thing in the piece. It is funny, sad and damning at once, and nobody drew
it. The ±σ tiles (war: VIOLENCE +4.2σ) name the logic of the piece: a word is known by how deviant it is. The spaces
are no longer arbitrary. There are fewer of them, they are labelled, and they are chosen by the data. The marked fuck
grid (SACRED 0.000 in blue, ACT: SWEAR in red) is the strongest single image: a form, stamped.

## What is still weak

- **The steps are still the showreel.** They are better argued but look the same: wireframe cubes, point clouds,
  plexus networks, contour maps. The ring of 24 rotating cubes is the Ikeda/Processing vocabulary circa 2012. At 4 m
  their labels ("EMOTION × CLOSENESS × COL…", truncated and overlapping) are noise. The centre space holds one idea
  per bar, and the ring dilutes it. The shallow part of the piece has become *better designed* but not *deeper*.
- **The verdicts collapse.** love and mother both end on EMOTION: TENDER, and nothing and to be or not to be both end
  on MATERIAL: VOID. On a category answer the dot plot is trivial: everyone sits at 0, the word sits at 1. The piece
  claims to show where a word stands, and on these endings it shows nothing.
- **The test list shows through.** spoon's neighbours on metal are KNIFE, RUST, **BITCOIN**. This is funny once, but
  a juror will read it as unfinished. The reference set is now the artwork's *politics* (who counts as normal), and it
  is still a placeholder.
- **The visitor is still not addressed.** The word is judged, not the person who typed it or the people watching.
- Craft: at the stand the nearest-word labels collide with their leader lines (i miss you, mother).

## Length

The artist is right that it feels short, but more steps would make it worse. **Keep the steps at ≤ 8 s. Give the time
to the two places where meaning lives.** (1) The fill/marked grid: hold it +1.5 s, long enough to read one absurd
finding. (2) The stand: 10–12 s instead of 6. The three nearest words arrive one at a time, each with its own tone,
the "chosen by the artist" line last, alone. Total around 22 s. Brevity in the spectacle, duration in the judgement.

## Is it exhibition-ready?

Nearly, for a thematic group show on classification (HeK, transmediale, Ars Electronica theme exhibition). It is not
ready for a solo room. Blocking: the real reference set; stand typography; a verdict rule that avoids trivial
category endings. Also a tested room (4.1 audio, a public keyboard, a safety moderation plan for what strangers type
in front of strangers).

## Top 5 changes, ranked

1. **Choose the 61 (or 200) reference words as a curatorial act, and say who they are.** A population (words from
   news headlines, from a dictionary's first page, from past visitors' *categories*, never their words) turns the norm
   into content. Name it on the stand.
2. **Make the stand the long, slow climax** (see Length): the neighbours revealed one by one, the sentence-long
   neighbours given room.
3. **Kill the rotating ring during the steps.** Leave the empty grid, the ghost of the form, or one static
   small-multiple row with legible labels. It is the single most derivative element.
4. **End on a continuous, contested answer, never a category.** Prefer the marked scalar with the highest σ *and*
   spread among the references (VIOLENCE, LOSS, SACRED), so no two common words share an ending.
5. **Address the visitor once.** For example, a last dim line before black: "YOUR WORDS WERE FILED UNDER: LOSS", or
   the room text changed after a submission. This is the move from words being measured to *being* measured.

**Score: 7/10** (round 8: 6.5). The argument is now on screen and the norm is visible. The "i miss you" neighbour
line is the kind of moment people retell. What holds it back is a middle section still borrowed from a familiar
aesthetic, and a reference set that isn't yet authored.
