# Round 10: five visitors (the grid, step-28+)

These visitors are imagined from the real frames: all 8 contact sheets, plus these full frames: war room / filled / stand,
fuck marked / network-full, mother tiles, love cloud, i miss you network / stand, spoon map-full, nothing axis. Each
word a visitor "types" is mapped onto one of the captured words.

## The visitors

**Maya, 16, with her dad. She types `fuck`.**
- "ACT: SWEAR 0.999, SACRED 0.000. It *gets* me." The marked grid goes straight into her story.
- Nearest on all answers: **LOOKS LIKE CRAP, RAGE, WAR**. She screams. Dad: "Who picked *looks like crap*?"
- The flashes are no longer painful: "it's just blinking." In the middle part she's on her phone.

**Hélène, retired nurse. She types `mother`.**
- The five warm tiles move her again. The ending this time is **EMOTION: TENDER 1.00**, with *baby, home, love* nearest.
  She's pleased: "That's right."
- The first and biggest tile still says **WHO: THEY +5.5σ**. She frowns, but it passes because the ending made up for it.
- She can't read the 24 little cubes from 2 m. "What are all the boxes for?"

**Sam, engineer, on a date. He types `love`; she types `i miss you`.**
- He's impressed by the cloud with *kiss, mother* labelled on stems: "that's actually a readable 3D scatter."
- Her ending, **LOSS 0.94**, nearest *why did you leave me without saying goodbye · loneliness · sorry*, lands hard.
  She goes quiet. That's the moment of the evening. But on the dot plot the long phrase is printed over GRIEF and the
  leader lines, so they can't read it where it matters.
- They'd type again to compare. He films the stand, not the cubes.

**Kenji, tourist with little English. He types `war`.**
- The grid is foreign, but the white bars pushed to the right edge read as "very", with no English needed.
- The red tiles and the big red **"war"**, with **VIOLENCE 0.98** far to the right beside *knife*, are clear.
  "Nearest on all 45 answers" is too small and too wordy for him.
- The rhythm feels heavy but it doesn't make him step back. He'd bring his kids, maybe.

**Jonas, art student, skeptical. He types `nothing`, then `spoon`.**
- **MATERIAL: VOID 1.00** is still the best line in the building. The contour map is pretty but it could be any word.
- The ring of 24 rotating cubes looks the same for every word: "Rack-mounted screensaver." Their labels collide
  (`EMOTION × CLOSENESS × COLEMOTION…`).
- The spoon stand lists **BITCOIN** as a near neighbour on metal. He laughs *at* it. Fix the reference list first.

## What they understood

- They understood why these answers were picked: the ±σ on the tiles, and their own line far from the crowd in each
  strip. Nobody reads "σ", but everyone reads "far from the others".
- **The final screen now works.** One big word, one human answer, and named neighbours. It's the most filmed frame.
  The weak points are label collisions (i miss you, love's *baby/mother/kiss* stacked at x ≈ 1) and the 0 end of the
  plot: a heap of anonymous dots with no names, so "how far" has nothing to compare against.
- **The middle is still the weak part.** The ring adds bulk, not meaning: 24 unreadable cubes turning at once. The only
  middle frames people remember are the ones with a named neighbour next to their word.
- **Strobe:** acceptable now. Nobody flinched. Keep the entrance notice.
- **Length:** it's right that it feels short, but the time should go to the ending, not the middle. People lean in
  at the stand and would stay 10 s. They'd stay 2 s longer on the marked tiles. They wouldn't stay longer in the cubes.

## Fixes, ranked

1. **Make the stand longer and let it build:** about 10 s. Label the dot plot one name at a time (nearest first, then
   2–3 far words at the 0 end, e.g. *spoon* on VIOLENCE), then the sentence. The answer becomes a comparison people
   can read out loud.
2. **Fix the stand's label layout.** Keep labels from colliding, give each leader line its own row, and cut long
   reference phrases to a line of their own at a larger size.
3. **Choose the real reference words now.** *Bitcoin, qwerty, looks like crap* undercut every ending. Visitors
   believe the neighbours more than the numbers.
4. **Hold the marked tiles one more bar**, and say the ±σ in words under each tile ("far above the others").
5. **Thin the ring.** Use 4–6 larger cubes (the triples that actually separate the word), with labels that fit and
   the word's cross named. Otherwise leave the ring empty on off-beats. 24 small cubes is noise at 2 m.
6. **Keep category slots (*who*, *act*) out of the lead tile**, not only out of the ending. Mother's first tile
   shouldn't be "they".
7. **Make the neighbours bigger in the middle.** When a space shows the word, give its 3 nearest one beat at stand size.
8. **Leave the last stand line faint in the room** until the next Enter, so couples compare.
