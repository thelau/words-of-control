## Repetition in a real room

The sequence answers the artist's worry, and the answer is yes. Look down the six rows: the columns are identical.
Grid → block of tiles → box with points → network in a box → white ridges → word on a dot plot. At room distance,
from the back of a crowd, the middle 10–12 s of every visitor looks the same: a white point cloud turning in a
wireframe cube. Colour doesn't help. Four of six rows are the same orange-red (the fallback accent for spoon,
father, bus, nothing), and love/war are the same red. Tempo and mood change, but a bystander can't hear 118 vs
132 bpm. After the third visitor the steps become wallpaper. After an hour they are a screensaver with a keyboard.

What should stay constant, as a ritual: the room with its breathing line, the fill with its 45 tones, the cut to
black. What must vary is **what the word earns**. Only the stand varies now, and it is the best thing in the piece:
"my father never said he was proud of me" → COMPLETELY SINCERE, IRONY 0.01, nearest HE NEVER CAME BACK;
"nothing" → SILENT, LOUDNESS 0.00, nearest EMPTY · END · NO. People will retell these, and photograph them.

## Thesis

The piece is about the machine's gaze reducing a confession to a coordinate. The stand shows exactly that, and the
flatness is devastating. The middle works against it. Ten seconds of Ikeda-grade dashboard *celebrate* the
analysis rather than exposing how little it holds. The critique lands in the ending despite the spectacle, not
because of it.

## The reference set as a statement

"Ordinary language" at 400 entries is a real position, a big step up from the test list. But it is still the
artist's English, Western, domestic ordinary ("king", "priest", "we will win"). That's fine if the wall says so.
Two issues show on screen:
- **Inconsistent counts.** The table says "48 WORDS", the cloud says "AMONG 399 WORDS", and the brief's stand
  line says "61 REFERENCE WORDS". A juror will notice.
- **Visitors will often type reference words** (love, mother, i miss you). Being measured against a norm that
  already contains you is a good idea. Say it: "ALSO ONE OF THE 400".

## Installation

A single-channel piece in a dark room of about 6 × 8 m. One projection 5–6 m wide at eye level (or an LED wall;
the hairlines need fine pitch). A keyboard on a low plinth 3–4 m from the screen, so the typist is watched from
behind. The public confession is the drama. Stereo pair plus sub, 75–80 dB peak, and the drone audible from the
corridor as the lure. One screen, not several: multiplying it turns it into signage. Wall text: title; "One
question to an AI that only judges. 45 answers. 400 ordinary words, chosen by the artist, as the norm. Nothing you
type is kept." Plus the list of the 400 printed small on the wall, so the norm can be read and argued with.

Before a show: frozen, credited reference set; caption counts fixed; a moderation stance for what strangers show
strangers at 5 m wide; an attract mode when idle (the 400 judged in turn, never visitors' words); a queue rhythm.
30 s per person plus typing means about 100 people an hour. Test with a day of real use. Plus hardware, and Jev
latency or outage.

## Score: 7/10

Stand-alone ending: 8.5. Everything before it: 6. The structure is sound. The middle hasn't found a reason to be

## Five changes, ranked

1. **Let the spectacle scale with how unusual the words are.** An ordinary word (spoon, bus late) gets one plain
   bar and goes straight to the stand. A rare one (father, war) gets the full four-bar crescendo. Repetition
   becomes meaning: the crowd learns that the machine only performs for outliers.
2. **Make the middle differ in kind, not parameters.** Pick 1–3 of the spaces per word from the data, in varying
   order. Drop the wireframe cube, the most generic image in the set. No two consecutive visitors should share a
   silhouette.
3. **Fix the colour collapse.** The orange fallback is on most words. Derive hue from other answers (temperature,
   light, valence) when Jev's colour is achromatic, and keep the accent for real exceptions.
4. **Freeze and publish the reference set.** Fix every count on screen to match it. Mark a visitor's word that is
   in the set.
5. **Give the stand more time and one line to the room.** Hold 12 s. After black, leave the answer word alone
   (SINCERE, SILENT) for a beat in the room state, so the next visitor starts in its shadow. Keep it in memory only
   and gone on the next Enter.
