# Round 13 — visitor: Marta (34, nurse, Saturday with a friend; her father died fourteen months ago)

Typed: "my dad died last year", "i'm happy today", "je t'aime".

## 1. What it seemed to say

**"my dad died last year"**. At first: lots of small charts, then nearly all of them went dark and a few white boxes stayed, so it picked out the heavy part and dropped the rest. After reading the labels: LOSS, SADNESS, PAST, WEIGHT, colour BLACK, then "SOMETHING OR SOMEONE GONE" in big letters. Yes. That is what it is.

**"i'm happy today"**. At first: yellow, so happy, fine, a smiley face. After reading: LIGHT, YELLOW, JOY, LONELINESS "LOW 4%", and it ends on "RISING / MOTION 0.97". Rising? I said *today*. I didn't say things are getting better.

**"je t'aime"**. At first: red and pink, so love, Valentine's Day. After reading: CLOSENESS, TENDER, SOFT, DISTANCE "LOW 2%", and it ends on "TENDER". Gentle and correct, but it's what anyone would expect.

## 2. Connected vs arbitrary

Connected:
- Most of the grid going black around the dad sentence. That felt like the right gesture, like the room going quiet.
- The ending of the first one: "SOMETHING OR SOMEONE GONE". Plain words, no jargon. I stood there a while.
- The nearest words "I LOST MY FATHER", and "MY MOTHER IS DYING" too. Other people have typed sentences like mine. That was oddly comforting.
- The PAST box being lit for my dad. In the happy one the grid also says TIME PRESENT 0.999, but it isn't picked out. I would have liked to *see* that switch from past to present, because that was the point of typing it second.
- "I STILL LOVE YOU" next to "je t'aime". Honestly I half meant that one for him, and that word "still" got me.

Arbitrary or wrong:
- "MY WIFE LEFT ME" as one of the three nearest to my dad dying. Being left is not the same as someone dying. It stung a bit.
- "TOP 3%" on LOSS and "TOP 2%" on WEIGHT. I understand it now (few words are that heavy), but at first it read like a ranking or a lab result, as if my grief came top of the class. "NEGATIVE 0.83" at the top looks like a test result too. As a nurse that number makes me flinch.
- My dad's sentence is coloured "BLACK", but the boxes are *white*. Clinical, hospital-white. I don't think that was meant to be.
- "RISING" as the last word for happy. Of all the boxes that one is the least me. JOY would have been better.
- The 3D cubes, the "ridges" and "TABLE · 45 ANSWERS × THE 48 NEAREST". Pretty, but they're for engineers. I stopped reading there and waited for the end.
- "KIND: LIVING BEING" for a sentence about someone who died. Odd, though maybe true.

## 4. Different enough?

Yes in colour: white, yellow, pink and red. The dad one was clearly darker and slower. Happy and je t'aime felt like the same performance in different colours, though: a positive word, a bright colour, everything at the far right of the line. Seen side by side, the sad one was the only one that felt like it had looked at *me*.

## 5. Enjoy it?

More than I expected, and I was wary of "AI judging my words". It helped that it says "ANSWERED BY AN AI" and doesn't pretend to be wise. The first performance moved me, mostly because of one plain line at the end. I'd type one more: "I miss his voice", to see if it finds HEARING, which it already marked for my dad, and NOSTALGIA, which it put at 0.80 but left dark.

One change: **say the ending in plain words for every piece, and pick the answer that's closest to what I said**, not the most extreme number. Also take the scores and percentages off the screens about feelings, or make them much quieter. "Something or someone gone" did more than any chart.
