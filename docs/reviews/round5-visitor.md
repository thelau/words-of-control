# Round 5 — first-time visitor

Source: `docs/captures/review7/sheet-*.png` (27 words). Stills only, no sound. I saw one frame per camera angle, so I'm judging the vocabulary and the pacing of the shots, not the motion.

- **spoon**: One particle field, then black. Boring, but honestly it's funny that the machine shrugs.

## Overall

**Coherent or a collection?** It holds together as one artwork. Same palette, same starburst "eye", same black at the end, and I believe it's one machine with one taste. The trouble is the vocabulary: there are about six images (wire tangle, particle city, particle field, ribbon/ring, relief slab, chladni membrane), and after ten words I can predict them. The colour tint does most of the work of telling words apart, and a tint alone isn't meaning.

**Most memorable image:** the **war** chladni rings, the white target that warps into a flower. The **grief** membrane is second.

**What bored me:** the neutral words (spoon, window, 42, afternoon, tuesday) all get the same distant particle carpet. The long emotional words also recycle the particle-city/landscape shot three or four times. rage, storm and thunder are the same piece in different colours. The starburst lens flare is in almost every shot, and it stopped meaning anything.

**What would keep me 20 minutes:**
1. Seeing that my word changed something only it could change. At the moment "love" and "baby" share a ring, and "rage" and "storm" share everything.
2. Short verdicts being *authored* and not empty. When spoon shrugs it should be a gesture, not a leftover.
3. A reward for exploring. Rare images I have to hunt for, like the chladni and the membranes, would get me typing "what does *this* make it do?"
4. Better pacing. Five shots of the same thing make it long. Three distinct shots would be better.
5. Something that answers back when I insult it. "looks like crap" came close.

The flashes before the verdict are the most Ikeda part and they look great. Sometimes I liked them more than the verdict itself.
