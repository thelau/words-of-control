# Round 6 — curator review (commissioning view)

Material: docs/captures/review9 (12 words) vs review7/8, references/craft, DECISIONS. Stills only: structure, not motion or sound.

## The work at its best

Not a visualiser: **a confessional for machine judgement**. You give a word, alone, at a distant typewriter. In front of everyone, a system that cannot feel *appraises* it and performs the verdict with a beauty it has not earned. Because "Jev only judges", the subject is classification (moderation, sentiment scoring, safety filters), the real condition of language online, not AI imagery. Keep *Words of Control*. The sensory part is the hook, not the meaning.

## Legibility

Not yet. The visitor sees their word, a flash of data, a line, grey dust, black. Nothing says *you were assessed*, and nothing ties the dust to the word. The appraisal is already exhibition-grade (fuck's cream chart, i miss you's bit field, the 42 barcode). But it lasts two seconds and hands off through **a horizontal line identical in all twelve sheets**. The 2D→3D "long echo" is the concept. It has to be seen happening, and today it is a cut.

## Repetition

1. **One template, refilled.** Every sheet runs word → card → line → N shots → black → cursor. Only the fill varies, and fill is the weakest carrier of difference.
2. **One substance.** About 80% of verdict frames are low-key grey bokeh. On a 6 m screen, joy, grief, rage and kiss will all read as "dim sparkle".
3. **Neutral words collapse.** 42, spoon and table all give the same white fin in dust.
4. **Dark words share set pieces.** rage, war and fuck all get cloud → cream Chladni → relief slab.
5. **Asymmetry.** fuck and war get about 20 frames. joy gets 3 shots and kiss a grey clay slab. **The machine rewards violence with spectacle**, and visitors will learn to type "war". If that is the thesis, make it deliberate. For now it reads as a budget allocation.

**Structural fix:** vary the *form*, not the fill. The appraisal chooses among several dramaturgies (duration, exposure, ending, how sound relates to image). Only the ritual stays fixed: type, Enter, black, cursor.

## Comparables

- **David Rokeby, *The Giver of Names* (1991–):** a machine names the objects placed before it. Rokeby's machine speaks its misreading. Yours must make the gap between word and verdict *felt*, without words.
- **Paglen/Crawford, *ImageNet Roulette* (2019):** classification turned on the visitor, legible and a little insulting. Without labels, you need the same sting through form: sometimes the machine is visibly cold or wrong about a tender word.
- **Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Room* (2006):** each trace joins and displaces the others', making a collective portrait. Your drone remembers, but your image forgets.

The warning is Anadol's *Machine Hallucinations*: data-sublime particles are now a lobby genre.

## Eight moves, ranked

1. **Appraisal becomes verdict in one unbroken shot.** Retire the line. The card the visitor just saw tilts into depth, and the camera dives in until their bars are the city and their bits are the lattice, with the sound held on the same tone. This is the concept made visible, and the piece's signature.
2. **Five dramaturgies, not one template.** *Shrug* (one overlit static take, 20 s). *Barrage* (8–15 hard cuts, strobing exposure). *Refusal* (staged as a scene, not an error path). *Misreading* (the verdict contradicts the word's obvious mood). *Echo without end* (for loss: black never quite comes, and the image decays over 60 s). The endings differ too.
3. **Exposure and scale as the main variables.** The cream appraisal frames are the strongest images in the set. Allow white-out, flooded colour, and extreme scale jumps (one grain vs a horizon). Every performance should have one poster frame readable from 15 m.
4. **Equal ambition for positive words.** joy, love and kiss need worlds as authored as Chladni and relief: heat, light filling the room, rising matter. If darkness is meant to earn more time, show that openly (e.g. a faint tally of seconds granted per mood).
5. **Spend the 49 answers on form, not just mood.** Material → substance, motion → camera, I/you/we → distance and scale, question → unresolved ending, confidence → false starts. Two words with the same valence must diverge visibly.
6. **Visible memory.** Keep no words, but let each verdict leave anonymous sediment in the rest image, so by evening the "empty" screen is a portrait of the day's judgements. The piece becomes durational and collective.
7. **Sound as architecture.** The typewriter stays acoustic and local, and the verdict plays in a multichannel field at the screen. The reverb tail travels back to the typewriter, so the room literally returns. Silence is a dramaturgy. Joy needs a physical register as strong as the sub.
8. **The space as an instrument of distance.** A real sensored typewriter with no paper (the machine keeps nothing), about 12 m from a floor-to-ceiling screen with a true black. Tune the timing so the visitor turns from the keys into the judgement.

## Verdict

Commission as a development. The concept is stronger than most AI works on the circuit, and the appraisal language is ready. It is not yet showable: the verdict is well-crafted fill inside one template, and legibility hangs on a line that means nothing. Do moves 1–3 first. They cost less than new clips and change what the work *is*.
