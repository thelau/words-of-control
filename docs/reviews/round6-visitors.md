# Round 6: simulated visitors (20-minute session)

Sources: `docs/captures/review9/sheet-*.png` (11 words) and `docs/captures/audio/*.wav` (EBU R128, spectrograms). For uncaptured words I used the closest captured one, marked →.

**Measured facts:**
- Loudness spans 30 LU. fuck is −10.4 LUFS and peaks at +0.1 dBFS (it clips). scream is −10.3, war −16, love −28.5, table −29, silence −41.
- Every appraisal closes on the same centred line with the same ~1.1 kHz tone at ≈2.2 s.
- spoon, table and 42 all open on the same edge-on "cross" landscape.
- rage, war and fuck share the line-star cloud and the Chladni sand. war and fuck also share the relief slab.
- joy carries four 20–110 Hz sub impacts (7–11 s). That's the dark words' vocabulary.

## Visitors

**Maya, 16, with two friends (min 0–5).** `hello` → spoon, then `does this work` → table. Both get a barcode flash, a grey cross and black. "Is it loading?" A friend types `fuck`. It's the loudest thing all day, the sub hits you in the chest, and the relief slab looks like cracked earth. `shit` → fuck again: the same line-stars, the same slab. "It did the same thing." A boy's name, `jake` → table. Then someone types a slur, which is blocklisted and gets a hard silent cut. Nothing tops the first swear, so they leave at minute 5. Out loud: "Do fuck again." They filmed one clip.

**Tom and Inès, a date (min 3–9).** `love`: number columns, the line, then 13 s of faint pink-green bokeh at −28.5 LUFS. "Is that it?" `kiss`: pink dust, then a lit relief that reads as skin. She likes it: "It's a close-up of skin." They each type the other's name and both get the grey cross (→ spoon). "We're both spoons." `baby` gets cold white wireframe prisms that look like architecture. They won't come back, but she'd post the kiss frame.

**Gérard, 71, alone (min 6–14).** `bonjour` → spoon. `afternoon` is long, quiet and warm, and he finds it "patient". He types `Margaret`, his late wife's name, and gets a neutral shrug (→ table). A long pause, then `I miss her` → grief: blue bars, then bokeh columns, a pale sheaf of light falling, a grid of threads like rain on a window, 18 s and cold. He stays in the black afterwards. The best moment of the session. Then he types `grief` itself: the same blue flash, the same line, the same rain grid. He leaves in silence, and he would return.

**Sara, art student (min 8–16).** `nothing`, `silence` (near-empty at −41 LUFS: "the only honest one"), `asdfgh`, then `war`. The cream spectrum flash is the one bright screen of the night. Then come a red-lit cube lattice, line-stars, the relief slab from four near-identical angles, and Chladni rings that warp like a wound. Then `rage`: line-star, Chladni and a red city again. "Same film, different trailer." By the third word she's counting the line+beep. She'd post one Chladni frame.

**Dev, sceptical engineer (min 12–20).** `42`: the cross, grey dust, black. "Hash to particles." `test` and `aaaa` → spoon. `joy`: a gold dust cube, a Chladni fold, a burst of gold rays. It's lovely, but he asks "why is joy threatening?" when four sub impacts land under the sand. `looks like crap` gets a quieter, irritated answer, and he respects that it answers back. He is bored after about 8 words: "Six generators and a colour grade." He won't post it, but he'll describe it at dinner.

## What lands
The swear's sub, felt in the body. The Chladni sand, where everyone paused. The grief rain-grid and the black after it. The cream flash (war, fuck). The kiss relief as skin.

## What fails
Names, greetings and "does this work" are about half of real inputs, and all get the grey cross. People read it as "not working". love, the most-typed word, gets the weakest verdict. The blocklist's silent cut reads as a crash. joy's subs contradict its gold picture. baby reads as architecture.

## Where repetition kills it
1. **Word 3:** the identical closing line + 1.1 kHz tone gets noticed and counted.
2. **Neutral streaks:** the same cross three times running.
3. **The second dark word:** line-star → Chladni → slab/city is the same edit, and the Chladni is spent on its second showing.
4. **Four views of one relief slab** inside a single verdict (war). People look away by the third.
5. **Near-synonyms and repeats** (fuck/shit, grief after "I miss her") give the same palette and flash, so the variety that exists goes unseen.

## Six changes, ranked
1. **Level the room.** Normalise verdicts to about −20 ±4 LUFS with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Carry intensity in density and sub, not gain. As it stands, the swear wins every session and love loses.
2. **Author names, greetings and questions.** Jev already reports the speech act. A name could be one point held alone in a vast field. A greeting could be the field turning to face you. "does this work" could get a fast appraisal and no verdict.
3. **Give love, kiss and baby a hero clip** as strong as the Chladni: warm, close and bright, with a swell and no sub. Keep sub impacts off positive words.
4. **Ration the rare clips globally.** Chladni and relief should appear about once in 5 performances, never twice in a row, and at most 2 angles per clip per verdict.
5. **Vary the appraisal's closing line.** Take its length, pitch and position from the word, or sometimes skip it. The ritual stays; the counter goes.
6. **Make sequences visible.** A repeated word, or a word that follows on from the previous one (Margaret → I miss her), should visibly inherit from it: the accent carried over, the drone audibly shifted. Also make the blocklist cut read as an intentional refusal (a held frame, a single tone). Then people type conversations instead of samples.
