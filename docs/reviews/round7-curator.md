# Round 7: curator review (does it feel like *this* word?)

Material: docs/captures/review13 (15 sheets), jev-readings.txt, and films of grief, shame, joy and maybe sampled at 3–4 fps. The films are separate runs from the sheets, so the shots differ.

## Per word

- **grief** (water · falling/contracting · dwindling): the best one yet. Vertical falling strands, then a thinning to near-black. Falling and dwindling read, and it feels like grief. In the film, though, the middle is a big radial fan identical to shame's, and there is no water shimmer.
- **shame** (fire/water · contracting 0.95 · strike): contracting is not visible. For 4 s the fan sits open and rotates slowly, then cracked slab, then drifting lines. Red flecks on grey read as "negative" but not as shame. Jev's strongest reading of the whole set (0.95) gets the weakest staging.
- **joy** (light · rising · swelling): warm glitter, a chandelier spiral, a rising rake. Light reads and it feels bright, but it is also generic celebration. Nothing swells into overflow.
- **love** (fire · spreading · steady): orange embers and spreading rays read as fire. A steady heartbeat would make it love, but the stills can't show it. The grey relief slab is foreign to it.
- **maybe** (smoke · drifting · dwindling): the clearest case of the new system working. Hazy horizontal smear, loose wandering threads, a fade. Undecided, soft, unresolved. Then a hard lattice and sand, which contradict it.
- **why** (void · strike 0.82): sparse grey dust, a thin wave. Void reads, but the strike (Jev's second-strongest reading) does not. *Why* should hit once and hang. It looks like maybe's quieter cousin.
- **fear** (ice · trembling · pulsing): blue-white needles, rain, a cold slab. Ice reads. Trembling can't be judged from stills and doesn't read in the sheet.
- **fire** (fire 1.00 · rising/spreading): embers, rising plumes, fans. The material is literal. Chladni sand and slab dilute it.
- **ocean** (water 1.00 · drifting · pulsing 0.90): blue bokeh, serpentine threads. It reads as water-ish, but a 0.90 pulse needs a visible tide, and the frames show none.
- **i miss you** (void/water · contracting · dwindling): cold vertical bars, reaching strands, a dark blur. The ache is there, and it is close to grief. That closeness is correct.
- **hello** (light · still · strike): four frames, one bright crossing, then gone. Greeting dramaturgy works. It's a nod.
- **war** (metal/fire · spreading/breaking · strike): starburst, shattered Chladni, falling columns, jagged slab. Breaking reads. It is still the longest and richest show (round 6 asymmetry).
- **forever** (stone · still 0.87): sweeping comet arcs and scattered bokeh, the opposite of still. It should be one unmoving mass that never ends.

## Verdict

Up from **4/10 (round 6) to 5.5/10**. Material now shows, but mostly as **tint and sprite** (orange embers, blue flecks, warm glitter) laid over the same shot library. Motion and rhythm, the two readings that carry what a word *means*, are almost invisible. Every word cycles the same repertoire of formations (fan/tube, lattice, cloud, slab, sand). **Grief and shame share the same fan, and so do joy and maybe.** A visitor would tell grief from joy, and probably maybe from why. Not grief from shame, and not joy from love. The mood axis still carries the difference. The new axes decorate it.

The root cause: material, motion and rhythm modulate shots chosen for other reasons. They should **choose and author** the shots.

## Six moves, ranked

1. **Let the top motion own the whole verdict.** One sustained gesture across all shots, big enough to read from 8 m: grief falls the whole time, shame contracts to a point and stays down, joy rises and doesn't come back, forever doesn't move at all. Camera angles still cut, but the gesture never resets. It is the single most legible carrier of meaning, and today it is a jitter inside a stock formation.
2. **Retire formations that contradict the reading.** A still word gets no arcs, a contracting word no open fan, a drifting word no rigid lattice. Filter the shot pool by motion and material *before* the seed picks: shame never gets the fan, maybe never gets the lattice, love never gets the grey slab. Fewer shots per word, all on-meaning.
3. **Make rhythm a composition and an audible event, not a modulation.** A *strike* is one hit, then held silence and a frozen image (why, shame, war). A *pulse* is a visible tide in brightness and density (ocean at 0.90). *Dwindling* thins the particle count and the sound together until one grain is left. *Swelling* overflows the frame. Today rhythm is too small to perceive. Scale it until a deaf visitor could tap it out.
4. **Use the second reading as the turn.** Jev's top two are the drama: shame is fire *and* water (burning, then drowned), grief contracts *after* it falls, war spreads *then* breaks. Stage the top reading in the first half, crossfade to the runner-up at a fixed point weighted by the probabilities. That turn is what separates grief from i miss you, and joy from love.
5. **Material as substance, not colour.** Give each material its own physics and optics instead of a tint: water refracts and sags, smoke has soft alpha and no hard edge, stone is one opaque mass, metal throws specular glints, light blooms past its edges. Remove orange/blue as the main signal so two negative words can no longer look alike apart from hue.
6. **Tie the monolith and sand to the word, or drop them.** The relief slab and Chladni plate now appear across words that have nothing in common (shame, love, fear, fire, war). Either the slab is carved by the material (cracked stone for forever, wet for grief, scorched for fire) and the sand pattern set by the rhythm, or each is kept for the one or two dramaturgies it serves. At present they are the strongest sign of "one template, refilled".
