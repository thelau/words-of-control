# Round 8: curator review (the grid)

Material: the 8 sheets in docs/captures/review/, full frames of war (filled, stand), love (marked), fuck (tiles),
spoon (network), mother (terrain full), i miss you (network full), to be or not to be (network full), nothing (room).
Read against GRID.md.

## What it now says

For the first time the piece has a thesis you can see. The **filled grid** is the work. Forty-five labelled cells
(hardness, sacred, loneliness, who, act), each with a value, and noise wherever the machine is unsure. It shows *the
questionnaire*, the form a word has to fill in to be known by a system. It is dry, a little absurd, and it is honest.
"war · order 0.067", "love · strangeness 0.002", "fuck · sacred 0.000" (marked, so the absence of the sacred is what
sets it apart): these are real findings about how a model files language. The same goes for the neighbours in the
spaces. *Spoon* is nearest to **baby, mother, money**. *I miss you* sits beside "why did you leave me without saying
goodbye". *Mother* is summed up at the stand as **"who: they 0.87"**. These are the moments I would remember, and they
are where the critique lives: the machine's taxonomy is confident, precise and slightly wrong. Nobody drew these
images, so the "Jev only judges" claim is now legible. You watch a form being filled in and a verdict being issued.
Nothing looks generated, because everything looks *administered*.

Mark → clear → one square → verdict → black is a good dramaturgy of reduction: 45 answers become 4–7, then one line.
That shape, a person reduced to their most deviant attribute, is the right subject for 2026.

## What is weak

- **The steps (7–9 s) are the shallow part.** Cloud, globe, terrain, lattice and ridges cycle at 150 bpm, 21 shots per
  word, and their choice is not driven by meaning. *Nothing* gets a globe and *mother* gets a lattice for no reason
  you can read. The ring of 24 thumbnails repeating the centre is busy, not insightful. This is where the piece turns
  into a showreel and loses its argument, just as the argument is building.
- **Ikeda.** The grid of sine test patterns, IBM-Plex-style mono labels, 1 px white on black, 25 ms test tones and
  point clouds in wireframe cubes are *data.matrix* / *datamatics* / *test pattern*, almost verbatim. Two things are
  genuinely yours: the **word's own colours** (the pastel mixes of love, mother and i miss you are un-Ikeda and
  good), and the **semantic content of the labels**. Ikeda's data is meaningless on purpose; yours means something.
  Lean on that. Every frame where the labels are unreadable (fast spaces, thumbnails) is a frame where you are just
  doing Ikeda.
- **The norm is invisible and thin.** "above 61 of 61 words": which 61? Being measured is always being measured
  *against* someone. Here the population is the artist's own test list, and the visitor cannot know that. It is the
  most important critical fact and it is hidden.
- **The visitor is not addressed.** The word is judged; the person who typed it is not. The piece is not yet about
  *being* measured, only about words being measured.

## Would I show it?

Yes, with the steps cut down. It belongs in a group show on classification and AI judgement (in the line of
Crawford/Paglen's *Training Humans*, a transmediale or HeK programme, an Ars Electronica theme exhibition), not in
an immersive "data spectacle" hall. It would lose there to Anadol. A **dark room of ~6 × 8 m**, one **5–6 m
projection or LED wall**, a single keyboard on a waist-high plinth 4 m from the image, and benches behind. The
audience watching a stranger's word being judged in public is half the work (confession, embarrassment, testing it
with "fuck"). Sound: **4 channels plus a sub**, the 45 test tones panned across the room as the cells land. The fill
should *surround* you like a form being stamped. Quiet room, no bleed from neighbours: the black and the drone need
silence.

## Recommendations, ranked

1. **Make the neighbours and the norm the climax (the one change).** Replace most of the 21 space cuts with **2–3
   slow spaces**, each held for a bar or two, whose only job is to place the word by name among the others (spoon →
   baby, mother, money). End the stand on the verdict *and* the company:
   `"spoon" · material: metal 0.99 · nearest: baby, mother, money`. Once, in the room text or at the stand, name the
   population: "61 words chosen by the artist". Measurement shown as relative, and its norm shown as arbitrary: that
   is the critique.
2. **Let the grid breathe and let its questions read.** The filled grid is the strongest image. Hold it ~1 s longer,
   and during mark/clear let the 4–7 surviving labels read at a size you can see from 4 m. The deviance ("sacred 0.000")
   should be *read*, not glimpsed.
3. **Choose spaces by meaning, or not at all.** If spaces stay, the data picks the form (a terrain only when there
   are two scalar axes, a network only for nearest words, a globe never unless the word is spatial). Drop the ring
   of repeats during the spaces; keep the ring as an empty grid, the ghost of the questionnaire around the verdict,
   as in war's stand frame, which is the best composed frame of the set.

Verdict: **6.5/10**, up from 5.5. The first version with a real idea on screen. It is currently buried under 8 seconds
of borrowed spectacle.
